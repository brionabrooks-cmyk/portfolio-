How I work

Strategy that moves organizations, not just campaigns.

I don't manage by headcount — I lead through alignment. My work lives at the intersection of customer insight, cross-functional influence, and commercial rigor. The programs I build aren't campaigns; they're operating systems that Sales, Product, and regional leaders can run long after I've moved on.

01

Diagnose before you build

Every program starts with a customer problem, not a channel plan. I use competitive intelligence, intent data, and Sales conversation analysis to find where real friction lives — then architect campaigns that speak to those pain points at the moment buyers are ready to act.

02

Align across every boundary

Pipeline doesn't come from marketing alone. I build shared accountability between Marketing, Sales, and regional leadership — co-owning targets and translating global strategy into locally executable programs. I've coordinated across EMEA, LATAM, and APAC at once, with no central reporting line in any region.

03

Build for reuse, not just launch

The best programs scale without me. I design modular campaign frameworks, repeatable playbooks, and reporting systems that give teams — including ones I don't manage — the structure to execute independently. Every playbook I've built has been adopted beyond its original scope.

Strategic approach

8-figure incremental pipeline in the first six months. The frameworks were adopted across all three regions as repeatable playbooks, cutting time-to-market for later launches. The broader demand program contributed 9-figure pipeline across my Salesforce tenure.

Intuit Mailchimp · B2B Marketing March 2026

DTC 2026: reinsert Mailchimp into the expert conversation

Event-Led DemandPipeline StrategyCompetitive PositioningCross-Functional Execution

The problem

Klaviyo owned the ecommerce mindshare conversation. Mailchimp had the product to compete but no presence in the rooms where DTC operators form opinions. The gap was presence and trust — which meant a brand-and-pipeline hybrid, not pure demand capture.

Strategic approach

I aligned Product Marketing, Sales, and Content around one program frame and led end-to-end execution — sourcing 7 DTC operators and investors with 400K+ combined reach and $500M+ in DTC revenue managed. Intuit Mailchimp · FY26 Q2 Jan–Feb 2026

ABM Sales Play #3: competitive conquest at account precision

ABMPaid SocialCompetitive StrategyMessaging Architecture

The problem

Mailchimp needed to convert Shopify merchants earning $750+ MRR away from Klaviyo — a high-LTV, loyal segment. The real challenge was message precision at account level: switching messaging that didn't feel generic, paired with ecommerce proof points credible to skeptical power users.

Strategic approach

I developed a two-track paid social brief: a DTC ecommerce track (Shopify integration ROI, automation proof) running parallel to a Klaviyo switcher conquest track ("Break Up With Klaviyo," 42% cost savings). LinkedIn used account-level personalization; Meta led with testimonial-anchored formats — designed as a modular, reusable system.

What it produced

The January 14 launch hit on schedule across both channels. 22 assets and 64+ copy lines delivered, with the creative framework adopted as a reusable switcher playbook carried into Q3. Executed within a multi-million-dollar B2B budget, with CAC tracked against pipeline contribution.
